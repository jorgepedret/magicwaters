The In-Person Program

Every Friday, 10am–3pm

Kids and grown-ups meet in the forest near Sooke, one day a week, all year. What we actually do that day depends on the season, the weather, the moon, even the time of day — we use the medicine wheel and the eight directions to shape the flow of a day, instead of a curriculum written months in advance.

Some Fridays are fort-building and animal tracking. Some are foraging, or fire, or just sitting still long enough to notice what's actually happening around you. Kids run a lot of their own games and make their own rules out there — that's what we mean by Children's Culture, the culture that shows up on its own when kids get real time together in a real place. Our job is mostly to protect the space for that, not to run the show.

Families come and go with the seasons of their own lives. New families are always welcome — come see a Friday before you decide anything.

Why This Exists

How it started

I started Magic Waters because I wanted my own kids to have real time outside, with other kids, without an adult running the show for them.

I also started Magic Waters because I wanted a possibility for the adults of the family to talk, to connect, to be with each other, while their children being held in an awesome space.

I've spent years living in intentional communities, doing this kind of work alongside other families. Magic Waters is what happened when I stopped researching it and just started doing it here, in Sooke, with the people around me.

It's still becoming what it's going to be. That's part of the point.

— Jorge, originator of Magic Waters

Don't Live Near Sooke?

Start your own where you are

We put together a practical guide for starting a self-run nature program with a small group of families — agreements, planning worksheets, and a way to get going with just three committed people. It's not a franchise, it's a starting point you adapt to your own place and your own kids.
